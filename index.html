How SFUSD assigns 6th-grade seats

Middle school in SFUSD is by application. There's no automatic neighborhood placement, so every family applies, including families already in SFUSD. Rules below are taken from SFUSD's own pages and were last checked on October 16, 2026.

Coming in 2028–29: neighborhood assignment for elementary school

On October 14, 2026 the school board voted 5-2 to replace the elementary lottery with address-based assignment starting in fall 2028, with citywide programs (such as immersion and some K-8s) still open by application. It doesn't change this year's middle school process, and coverage so far describes it as an elementary change only. Two possible knock-on effects for middle school later: SFUSD said in 2022, for an earlier version of the zone plan, that feeder patterns may need adjusting as elementary zones change, and the plan floats turning two or three citywide K-8s into K-5 neighborhood schools. The final policy vote is scheduled for April 13, 2027.

Sources: SF Standard · KQED · SF Standard (options explained)

1 · You have to apply

Students are not automatically enrolled at their feeder middle school. Submit an application by the Main Round deadline (February 1, 2027) to be considered in the first round.

2 · List your feeder school

Every SFUSD elementary school has a feeder middle school. SFUSD says applicants who list their feeder middle school "typically all" get in, and strongly recommends including it alongside your other choices. It's your safety net.

3 · Rank in true preference order

You're assigned to your highest-ranked choice that has space. Adding more schools lower on your list never hurts your chances at the ones above it, so list every school you'd accept, favorite first.

When a school has more applicants than seats

Tiebreakers decide who gets the seats, in this order. A student in an earlier group gets in before anyone in a later group.

  1. 1. Sibling
    A brother or sister enrolled at, and continuing at, the requested school.
  2. 2. Middle school feeder
    Currently enrolled at an SFUSD elementary school that feeds the requested middle school.
  3. 3. Bayview elementary
    Attended Carver, Drew, Harte or Malcolm X for K–5; applies to any SFUSD middle school.
  4. 4. Test score area (CTIP1)
    Lives in a census tract with the city's lowest average test scores.
  5. 5. Staff
    Child of a staff member at the school.
  6. 6. Random number
    Everyone else is ordered by lottery.

Willie Brown Middle School adds a 94124 ZIP code tiebreaker right after the Bayview elementary tiebreaker.

Applying to a language pathway

Students must first meet the pathway's eligibility requirements. Elementary immersion and biliteracy programs continue as a Secondary Dual Language Pathway for students already proficient in the language; a Secondary World Language Pathway is open to all students. Tiebreaker order for pathways: language pathway sibling → language pathway continuation → sibling → middle school feeder → CTIP1 → staff → random.

What about the new neighborhood-school plan?

On October 14, 2026 the school board voted to replace the elementary lottery with address-based assignment starting in the 2028–29 school year. Applications this fall for 6th grade in 2027–28 use the current rules described here.

    Website Data Sources & Methodology

    Status as of Oct 16, 2026: every news link was opened and checked. For public and charter schools, enrollment, demographics, suspensions, chronic absenteeism, test scores and growth are official state data; admission odds, feeder schools and language pathways come from SFUSD's own published data. Private-school tuition, aid and ratios were checked on each school's website and are labeled school-reported. Program descriptions, bell times, Muni lines, arts and sports lists and high-school destinations have not been verified.

    Intended sources:

    • CAASPP state test results (official, 2025–26): Reading and math scores for every public and charter school, grades 6–8 only, overall and by student group, from the CDE/ETS research files. K-8 schools are compared on their grade 6–8 students only. Groups under 11 students aren't reported by the state. Private schools don't take this test.
    • Student growth (official, 2025 CA Dashboard): How much each school's students improved compared with students statewide who had similar scores the year before, for reading and math, overall and by student group. Weighted average of growth from spring 2023 to spring 2025, grades 4–8. From the CDE growth data file; plain-language labels map to the state's five levels (Minimal, Moderate, Average, Accelerated, Exceptional Growth).
    • CA Dept. of Education data files (2025–26): enrollment and demographics (census day; grades 6–8 shares), suspension rate (all students) and chronic absenteeism (grades 7–8). Student-teacher ratio = 2023–24 enrollment ÷ teacher FTE from the NCES Common Core of Data; it counts all teachers, so classes are larger. Private enrollment comes from the 2025–26 private school affidavit.
    • SFUSD Enrollment Center: 2026–27 school list (feeder schools and language pathways), March 2026 requests per seat, and Main Round success rates 2023–27 (admission odds by tiebreaker).
    • Local Press (2023–2026), link-checked: The San Francisco Standard, The Frisc, Mission Local, and SF Chronicle. These also feed the Momentum signal.
    • Private school websites (checked Oct 16, 2026): tuition, share of families on aid, ratio, class size and students of color, as each school reports them; each profile links its source.

    What is School Scale & Teaming?

    Large Comprehensive (900+ students) & "House" Model: Schools like AP Giannini and Presidio divide 6th graders into dedicated "Houses" or "Pods" of ~90 students with shared core teachers to ensure an intimate community within a large campus.
    Mid-Sized Middle Schools (450–899 students): Balanced neighborhood hubs like Roosevelt, Marina, Francisco, James Lick and Denman.
    Small Schools (under 450 in grades 6–8): K-8 and independent campuses with continuity across elementary and middle grades, plus small comprehensive middle schools (Everett, Willie Brown, MLK, Visitacion Valley). Enrollment figures are from the CA Dept. of Education (2025–26); K-8 totals include all grades.

    How the Momentum score works

    Momentum asks one question: is recent local press about this specific school getting better or worse? It is shown next to the Fit Score but never changes it — Fit is about your child, Momentum is about the school's direction.

    • Only school-specific stories count. District policy (algebra, schedules, the lottery) affects every school, so it isn't credited to whichever school a reporter happened to mention.
    • Each article is hand-labeled positive, mixed or negative for each school it's about, and weighted 1 if the school is the subject or 0.5 if it's a featured example.
    • Recent coverage counts more: full weight under 12 months, half weight at 12–24 months, nothing after that.
    • Follow-ups count once. Two articles on the same incident are one story.
    • Result: a total of +1 or more shows as Getting better; −1 or less as Getting worse. Smaller totals show as Some good news, Mixed press or Some concerns. Schools with nothing written about them show no badge.

    Read it with care: most schools get no coverage at all, and press gravitates to scandals and turnarounds. "No coverage" is not a neutral rating — it means there's nothing to go on. Check each school's evidence list before drawing conclusions.

    How the FIT Score is Calculated

    The Fit Score is computed out of a strict 100-point budget:

    • Student Personality Archetypes (up to 30 pts): Based on classroom sensory load, scale, and literature/writing passions.
    • Favorite Subjects & Passions (up to 30 pts): Matched electives (Band, Robotics/STEM, Dual Language, etc.).
    • Scale & Teaming Structure (up to 15 pts): Alignment with mega, mid-sized, or compact cohorts.
    • Location & Transit Quadrant (up to 15 pts): Neighborhood proximity.
    • High School Trajectory (up to 10 pts): Alignment with Lowell, SOTA, or independent prep tracks.
    • Base Curriculum Standard (5 pts): Core academic baseline.
